# Common mistakes in the OpenAI interview

The mistakes that candidates report most often in the OpenAI loop concern format, not knowledge. Candidates prepare for algorithm puzzles and meet a practical build, or they prepare a backend diagram and meet a product question. This page lists ten such mistakes and what to do instead.

## The ten mistakes

| # | Mistake | Round | What to do instead |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Preparing only algorithm puzzles | Coding | Practice timed builds that you then extend |
| 2 | Chasing the optimal solution first | Coding | Finish a simple correct version, then improve it |
| 3 | Writing no tests | Coding | Write test cases before the time ends |
| 4 | Waiting for hints | Coding | State what you are trying, then ask |
| 5 | Opening a product design with services and storage | System design | Sketch the screen, then the API, then the schema |
| 6 | Naming a technology you cannot defend | System design | Name the property you need |
| 7 | Designing everything at the same depth | System design | State the scope and choose one deep section |
| 8 | A generic answer to "Why OpenAI?" | Behavioral | Name a specific product and connect it to your work |
| 9 | Enthusiasm in place of a view on AI | Behavioral | Prepare a reasoned position on direction and risk |
| 10 | Saying "we decided" in the project round | Project walkthrough | State what you personally decided, with numbers |

## Coding mistakes

**1. Preparing only algorithm puzzles.** Sources estimate that puzzle practice alone covers about half of what backend candidates face. The other half is practical work, such as a parser, an API client, or a ledger that grows.

**2. Chasing the optimal solution first.** A half-finished optimal solution is reported to score worse than a complete simple one, so finish the correct version before you improve it.

**3. Writing no tests.** Test coverage is reported as a stated grading criterion. Most candidates skip tests under time pressure.

**4. Waiting for hints.** Candidates report less help than at other large companies, so state each attempt aloud before you ask for a hint.

## System design mistakes

**5. Opening with services and storage.** On product questions, interviewers are reported to redirect candidates to the user experience, and the redirection costs time. Experienced backend engineers are reported to make this mistake most often.

**6. Naming a technology you cannot defend.** Saying a product name invites a full comparison against its alternatives. Naming the property, such as a durable log, keeps the discussion on your design.

**7. Designing everything at the same depth.** You cannot design a chat application in one hour. State what you will cover first and why, and then go deep on one component before the interviewer chooses one for you.

## Behavioral and project mistakes

**8. A generic answer to "Why OpenAI?".** The question is reported in up to three conversations. If your answer still works with another company's name in it, rewrite it. Pay or brand should not be the substance of the answer.

**9. Enthusiasm in place of a view on AI.** Interviewers ask where AI is heading and where it could go wrong. Sources report that a position with a reason scores better than excitement. Prepare one example of a risk and what you would do about it.

**10. Saying "we decided" in the project round.** Vague ownership invites the question "what did you decide?". Choose a project where you made the decisions, and bring request volume, latency, data size, and team size. A small project needs a credible account of what would fail first under very large demand.
