# The OpenAI behavioral interview

OpenAI is commonly reported to spread behavioral evaluation across the loop instead of holding one deciding culture round. You meet behavioral questions in the recruiter screen, in dedicated team fit conversations, and inside technical rounds through the judgment you show. The questions return to five themes: motivation, speed with incomplete information, ownership, disagreement, and failure.

## How OpenAI frames the round

OpenAI's stated mission is to ensure that artificial general intelligence benefits all of humanity. Sources describe the culture as intense, with high expectations and a fast release pace. The behavioral conversations check whether you would do well in that environment and whether you understand it before you join.

Two question types are specific to this company.

- **Motivation.** "Why OpenAI?" is reported in the recruiter screen, with the hiring manager, and often again in the final loop.
- **A view on AI.** Interviewers ask where AI is heading, where it could go wrong, and how you think about the risks of your own work.

Sources report that a view of your own, with a reason behind it, scores better than enthusiasm. A considered answer about risk and responsibility is reported to distinguish senior candidates.

## Question themes

| Theme | Reported question | What a strong answer shows |
|---|---|---|
| Motivation and mission | Which OpenAI product or research direction interests you most, and why? | A specific product or direction tied to your own work |
| AI risk and safety | How do you think about the risks of the technology you would build here? | A reasoned position, with one concrete example |
| Speed and ambiguity | Tell me about a release under a tight deadline. What did you cut, and how did you decide? | A scope decision with numbers and a date |
| Ownership | Tell me about a problem you fixed before anyone asked you to. | A decision you made alone and its result |
| Disagreement | Tell me about a time you disagreed with your manager. | What you did and how it was resolved |
| Failure | Tell me about a significant mistake. What did you learn? | The change you made afterward |
| Work across functions | Tell me about working with a legal team or another function outside engineering. | How you adapted to a non-engineering constraint |

## How to answer "Why OpenAI?"

A structure in three parts is commonly recommended.

1. Name the specific thing that attracts you: a product, a research area, or how research becomes a product. Use two or three sentences.
2. Connect it to your background: what you built and the scale you handled. Use three or four sentences.
3. State what you want to work on in this role. Use one or two sentences.

Test the answer by replacing the company name with another AI lab. If the answer still works, it is too generic.

## How to prepare stories

STAR is a structure for behavioral answers. It stands for Situation, Task, Action, and Result. Sources recommend adding a short final part on what you learned, because the failure questions ask for it directly.

- Prepare five to eight stories that cover speed, ownership, conflict, failure, and scale.
- Write each story down and keep it under two minutes.
- Put numbers in every story: the timeframe, the size of the change, and the measured result.
- Use "I" for decisions and "we" for credit.
- Do not memorize a script. Interviewers ask follow-up questions on details, and a recited answer fails under them.
- Read OpenAI's charter and recent product announcements, so that your motivation and risk answers refer to real work.
