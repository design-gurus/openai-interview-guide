# The OpenAI interview process

The OpenAI engineering interview is commonly reported to run in five stages after the application. They are a recruiter or hiring manager call, a practical coding screen, an early system design interview for some roles, a final loop of four to six interviews, and team matching. Candidates commonly report four to eight weeks once interviews begin.

Formats differ by team and change over time, so ask the recruiter what your specific loop contains.

## The stages in order

| Stage | Format | What it decides |
|---|---|---|
| Application | Resume review, with a reply reported in about one week | Whether a recruiter contacts you |
| Recruiter or hiring manager screen | A call of about 30 minutes | Whether your background and motivation fit the role |
| Technical screen | 45 to 60 minutes of live coding in a shared editor | Whether you write working, readable code and explain it |
| Early system design | 45 to 60 minutes, for some roles | Whether you can structure a practical design and answer follow-up questions |
| Final loop | 4 to 6 interviews with 4 to 6 people, 4 to 6 hours over 1 or 2 days | The hiring decision |
| Team matching and offer | Calls with one or more teams | The team you join and your level |

## What happens in each stage

**Recruiter or hiring manager screen.** The recruiter asks about your background, the work you want next, and why you chose this company. The hiring manager version goes further into what you built and decided in recent projects. "Why OpenAI?" is reported here and again in later rounds.

**Technical screen.** You write code that runs. The task is usually a small piece of real software, and the interviewer extends the requirements after the first version works. Some teams are reported to use a take-home task (a project you complete alone before a deadline) or a skills assessment instead. See [Coding interview](coding-interview.md).

**Early system design.** Some roles add a design round before the final loop. It tests whether you can structure a problem, and the round inside the final loop then tests depth. See [System design interview](system-design-interview.md).

**Final loop.** Most candidates complete this stage remotely. A typical engineering loop is commonly reported to include the following rounds.

- Coding rounds with more scope than the screen, often a small working system that grows.
- A deeper system design round with harder follow-up questions.
- Behavioral and team fit conversations about ownership, speed, and working with others.
- For some loops, a project walkthrough of 45 to 60 minutes with a short slide deck.

**Team matching and offer.** After a pass, you speak with one or more teams. These calls decide your team and your level, so treat them as part of the evaluation. See [Levels](levels.md).

## Reported waiting times

| Step | Commonly reported wait |
|---|---|
| Recruiter screen to technical screen | A few days to one week |
| Technical screen to result | About one week |
| Scheduling the final loop | One to three weeks |
| Final loop to decision | About one week |
| Decision to written offer | A few days |

Positive results are reported to arrive sooner, often two or three days after the final loop. Rejections are reported to take two to four weeks and to contain little feedback. If you hear nothing for more than one week after a stage, send one short message to the recruiter.

## What is consistent across stages

- Interviewers are reported to prefer solid engineering and clear explanation over clever tricks.
- Interviewers have freedom to choose questions, so there is no fixed question bank.
- Test coverage is reported as part of the stated grading criteria.
